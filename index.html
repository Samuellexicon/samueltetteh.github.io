Three Guiding Principles

Enduring Power through Covenant Keeping and Deep Faith

Lasting power, whether spiritual or professional, comes only through deep and genuine rootedness rather than surface-level preparation. Just as an empty vessel cannot withstand the energy directed at it, shallow technical knowledge and shallow faith will both fail under pressure. Keeping covenants is the channel through which divine power flows into daily decisions and professional work. This principle shapes how I approach every technical challenge: depth is not optional, it is the difference between a system that holds under load and one that fails at the worst possible moment.

"He is like a man which built a house, and digged deep, and laid the foundation on a rock." — Luke 6:48

Co-Creation with God through Revelation and Intentional Planning

Effective work begins with spiritual creation before physical execution. The Lord created all things spiritually before they were naturally upon the face of the earth. This pattern is not incidental; it is the Lord's own method. When planning is done in partnership with God — through prayer, scripture study, and seeking personal revelation — it becomes an act of co-creation that aligns our work with His purposes. This principle has reshaped how I approach every significant project, from cloud architecture proposals to this capstone itself.

"For I, the Lord God, created all things, of which I have spoken, spiritually, before they were naturally upon the face of the earth." — Moses 3:5

Disciple-Leadership as a Moral and Relational Calling

Leadership in every domain is always a moral act with real consequences for real people. As a future systems administrator, I will hold in my hands the security, reliability, and privacy of the systems that people and organizations depend on. That is not a neutral technical responsibility — it is a moral one. The three questions Elder Clark posed guide every professional role I enter: What is my purpose? How can I lift and strengthen those around me? How can I add value?

"That which is of God is light; and he that receiveth light, and continueth in God, receiveth more light." — D&C 50:24
Reflective Activity

Faith and Technology: Personal Reflections

On Building Something That Outlasts the Present Moment

During a recent period of quiet reflection and pondering, I found myself thinking about legacy — what am I building, and will it outlast me? In my electrical maintenance work at Volta Aluminium, I maintain systems that keep a major production facility running. If I do my job well, those systems continue to function reliably long after my shift ends. In IT, the same principle applies at a larger scale. The infrastructure I design, the security policies I implement, and the documentation I write will shape the environments that future administrators inherit.

The temple, as a place where eternal covenants are made and families are sealed across time, is the ultimate expression of building something that outlasts the present moment. This realization deepened my sense of professional purpose in ways I did not anticipate. The discipline of spiritual and professional growth that this course has cultivated must become self-sustaining after graduation. I cannot take for granted the habits of prayer, study, and covenant keeping that have made this season of growth possible — just as a well-maintained system must be actively monitored and updated rather than left to run without oversight. The integration of my spiritual and professional identities is not a project that ends with this course. It is the ongoing work of a disciple-leader.
